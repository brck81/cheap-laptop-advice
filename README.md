# Best Cheap Laptop: How to Pick a Sub-$400 Machine That Still Works in Three Years, and Pay Less at Checkout

Cheap laptops got harder to shop for, and it isn't your imagination.

The memory crunch that started with AI data centers hoovering up the world's DRAM and NAND supply lands hardest on entry-level machines, because cheap laptops already spend the biggest share of their build cost on memory. The practical outcome, as budget-laptop trackers have documented through 2026, is ugly: configurations that sold for $429 with 16GB of RAM in 2024 now ship with 8GB at the same price, and several previously recommended models have simply left the market. One 2026 roundup noted that every single pick from its previous edition had gone unavailable or 404'd.

So the question "what's the best cheap laptop" doesn't have a single answer this year. It has a better one: which compromise can you live with, and how do you avoid paying the sticker price for it.

## What your money actually buys right now

PCMag's under-$500 guide puts the realistic ceiling at Intel Core 3 or Core 5 (or i3/i5) and AMD Ryzen 3 or Ryzen 5 for Windows machines. A "5"-class chip under $500 is a genuine win. Intel's new Core Series 3 "Wildcat Lake" parts and Qualcomm's Snapdragon C line are aimed at exactly this bracket, but neither is guaranteed to appear under $500 yet.

Memory is where the arguments start. PCMag's position is that you should never buy a Windows machine with less than 8GB, and that 4GB is best avoided outside a basic Chromebook. Newegg's back-to-school guide for 2026 goes further and calls 16GB the floor. Both are defensible. 8GB runs Windows 11 fine for a browser, a document and a video call. 16GB is what keeps the machine comfortable in year four, when the browser has more extensions and the OS has more background services. If a 16GB config costs $40 more than the 8GB one, take it.

Storage is the other quiet trap. 128GB is at the high end of this bracket, and a good chunk disappears into the OS. You'll see eMMC and UFS instead of a proper SSD at the very bottom, which is usable on a Chromebook and noticeably slow on Windows. If you can find 256GB or 512GB at the same price, that's the deal worth chasing.

The screen is the compromise you can't fix later. A 1080p IPS panel is the difference between a laptop you use and a laptop you tolerate; 1366×768 at 15.6 inches looks soft the moment you open a spreadsheet. Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5 are common even on cheap machines, so that's one area where you rarely have to give ground.

## The refurbished route, if you can handle older silicon

Under $400, the used business-laptop market is genuinely strong. Machines that sold new for $800 to $1,200 between 2019 and 2022 now land in the $160 to $400 band with specs that beat anything new at the price. One refurbisher's July 2026 pricing put a Dell Latitude 5490 or 5500 with an i5-8250U and 16GB at $170 to $260, a Lenovo ThinkPad T490 at $200 to $320, an HP EliteBook 840 G5 or G6 at $160 to $240, and a 2019 or 2020 Intel MacBook Air at $280 to $380.

The rules for buying in that market are short. Skip anything under 8GB of RAM, skip spinning hard drives, and skip ungraded stock unless you can inspect the machine yourself. Buy the 2020 MacBook Air rather than the 2018 or 2019 model if you want Apple, purely because the scissor-switch keyboard replaced the butterfly mechanism.

3. Accept all cookies on the retailer's site. All three manufacturers can only confirm the transaction if their cookies are accepted.
4. Disable ad blockers, and don't click external ads, other cashback extensions or coupon sites during the session.
5. Use only promo codes listed on the cashback platform's merchant page. All three manufacturers explicitly exclude orders placed with vouchers or codes they don't list.
6. Complete one order per click-through. Two orders means two trips back to the platform.
7. Finish on the same device you started on. Don't begin on the website and pay in the app.
8. Shop in your own market. Buying from another country's version of a store is one of the reliable ways to lose the cashback.

And the promo-code rule is a genuine trade-off: an unlisted 10% off code will usually save you more than 1.5% cashback, so read the merchant's terms and pick the larger number.
